Avenmere Seed Vault maintains teaching collections of dormant seeds beside an old market garden. Packets on the public wall are empty examples; viable material stays in labelled storage drawers.

The red density float at Avenmere Seed Vault is kept in the narrow side drawer of the weighing bench. The brass seed scoop is hung on a hook at the wash station. Neither tool is part of the public packet display.

Avenmere Seed Vault wraps the paper seed envelopes in a loose linen sleeve because sharp wooden drawer joints can abrade their edges during removal. The sleeves protect the paper rather than regulating germination.

At Avenmere Seed Vault, Miren Oake assembled the first humidity cabinet on 23 April 2012. The booklet Seed Rooms Explained was written by Cora Belth and published on 15 February 2017.

Avenmere Seed Vault retains germination logs for 43 months. Logs associated with an unresolved provenance claim are retained until the claim is settled. The rule applies to germination logs, not donor correspondence.

Purchases above 8,400 credits at Avenmere Seed Vault need written authorization from the Vault Keeper and the Seed Records Officer. The purchasing notice supplies no approval rule for purchases at or below 8,400 credits.

For the Avenmere Seed Vault material store, July has the highest monthly mean temperature: 24 degrees Celsius. The drawer room and the teaching garden are separate monitoring locations.

The dormant-seed teaching drawer at Avenmere Seed Vault contains 156 packets divided equally among twelve labelled compartments. Each compartment belongs to one teaching variety, not one growing season.
