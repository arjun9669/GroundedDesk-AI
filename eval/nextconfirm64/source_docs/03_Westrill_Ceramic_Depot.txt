Westrill Ceramic Depot stores kiln furniture and fired reference pieces from a former tile cooperative. The loading office overlooks the yard, and the public study shelf is beside the entrance.

At Westrill Ceramic Depot, the silver firing cone is on the upper shelf of the locked measurement cupboard in the loading office. It is a reusable metal reference shape, distinct from the expendable clay cones used inside kilns.

Westrill Ceramic Depot keeps the porous sample bricks in slotted wooden racks because trapped condensation can stain their unglazed faces. The gaps let air circulate between adjacent bricks.

Westrill Ceramic Depot commissioned its first kiln on 8 August 2008 and its second kiln on 21 May 2012. Its third kiln was commissioned on 14 March 2017. The third drying rack was installed on 2 October 2018 and is not a kiln.

The glazing guide for Westrill Ceramic Depot was written by Dena Rusk and published on 6 December 2019. A handwritten workshop copy dated 2018 was a draft, not the published guide.

Westrill Ceramic Depot retains dispatch ledgers for two years. If a breakage claim remains unresolved, keep the affected ledger until the claim closes. The note does not address customs appeals or insurance investigations.

The quarterly monitoring sheet for Westrill Ceramic Depot lists a highest monthly mean temperature of 29 degrees Celsius in September. The sheet does not identify the room or storage area where that monthly temperature was measured.

The reference kiln at Westrill Ceramic Depot holds 48 test tiles on six equal shelves. Forty-eight is the full-load capacity; damaged tiles are not included in the reference load.
