Nettleford Map Gallery occupies two floors of a former surveying office. Wall displays use reproductions; originals are inspected at supervised tables in the reading room.

The folded river chart at Nettleford Map Gallery lies in flat drawer 4 under the supervised reading table. An enlarged reproduction hangs in the entry corridor, but the corridor copy is not the original chart.

Nettleford Map Gallery interleaves the coastal sheets with unbuffered tissue because alkaline packing can alter their sensitive purple annotation ink. Ordinary route diagrams without that ink use a different packing stock.

Nettleford Map Gallery commissioned the first survey cabinet on 9 July 2011, the second on 16 April 2015, and the third survey cabinet on 3 November 2019. The smaller display cabinet opened on 11 February 2021; opening the display cabinet was not commissioning a survey cabinet.

At Nettleford Map Gallery, Arel Venn restored the southern estuary panorama on 22 August 2014. Joelle Sorn wrote the Panorama Notes booklet, published on 10 March 2016. A publicity poster issued in 2015 was not that booklet.

At Nettleford Map Gallery, loans exceeding 62 map sheets require written authorization from the Collections Registrar and the Paper Conservator. Exhibition reproductions are counted separately and do not enter the map-sheet loan total.

The highest monthly mean temperature recorded in the Nettleford Map Gallery material store is minus 3 degrees Celsius in January. The material store is chilled; the reading room is maintained at ordinary working temperature.

A public reading session at Nettleford Map Gallery lasts 75 minutes, including a ten-minute settling interval before staff handle the originals. Readers receive the full session even if no original is brought out.
